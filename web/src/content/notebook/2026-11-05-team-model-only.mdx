## Two ways to get a final score

The project has two ways to reach a final score:

- **Team model.** Two gradient-boosted regressors, one for the home score and one for the away score, trained on the 21 team-level inputs.
- **Player model.** A separate model predicts points for each player, and the team total is the sum over the expected lineup.

The published forecast used to blend the two, along with a third model that predicted the margin directly. The question was whether the blend helped.

## Test

Each layer was turned off in turn, and the forecasts were re-run out of sample on three slices: 116 games of 2026-27 and 411 games from February to June 2026, which split into February to April and the playoffs.

## Why the player model hurt

Summed player predictions came out 20 to 60 points below real team totals. The likely reason is that it predicts each expected player separately, so points from players outside that lineup, garbage time and overtime are missed. Blending it in helped only when that low bias happened to cancel a bias in the team model. That is luck, not signal.
