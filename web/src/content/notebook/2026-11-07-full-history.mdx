## Question

Elo ratings and the training data both started in October 2023. That kept the model focused on recent basketball, but it threw away two seasons of games that the database already had. Is more history better, or does older basketball (a different pace, different rules emphasis, different rosters) just add noise?

## Why more history need not mean older basketball

Two mechanisms keep recent games in charge even with a longer history. First, training weights decay with age. Each game's weight falls off exponentially with the days since it was played, halving about every 12.5 months, so a game from three seasons ago counts for about 13% as much as last night's.

Second, Elo pulls every rating 25% of the way back toward 1500 each summer, so a 2021 rating has largely faded by 2024.

What the longer history adds is earlier Elo ratings (by 2023 every team has a well-settled rating instead of starting from 1500) and more examples of rare situations: elimination games, long injury absences, extreme rating gaps.

## Check

Both start dates were trained and compared on the same held-out seasons:

| Held-out season | Winners picked, 2023 start | Winners picked, 2021 start |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-26 | 65.3% | 67.0% |

Margin and total errors were lower with the longer history in two of the three test seasons (2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27 so far).

## Change

Elo ratings and training data now both start in October 2021. The nightly forecast replays the same games as training, so the ratings the model sees each night are the ones it learned from.
