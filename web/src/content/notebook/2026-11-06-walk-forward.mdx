## Why a backtest

A few weeks of live forecasts cannot tell you much. Thirty games can show 70% or 55% winners just by chance. To learn how good the model really is, it has to be tested on thousands of games, and the only way to get that many is to go back in time. The danger in going back is leakage: using anything the model could not have known on the day of the game.

## Data work first

Three gaps had to be closed before the backtest could be trusted:

- **209 days of 2022-23 official injury reports** were missing. Without them the availability input would fall back to "who did not play", which is only known after the game.
- **260 games had no box score**, which breaks the rotation definitions and recent-form inputs.
- **Team form had to go back to 2021** so the first backtest season had a full ten-game history.

## Caveats

- These are **retrospective** tests, not forecasts published at the time. The site's record merges them with the forecasts the daily pipeline saved before tip-off (one forecast per game, the live one when it exists), and the Performance page reports the live forecasts on their own as well.
- Ten games where the source table stores the same team as home and away are left out of the published figures; see [Methodology](/methodology/).
