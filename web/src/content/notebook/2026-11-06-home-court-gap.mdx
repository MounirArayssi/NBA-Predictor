## Question

The model applies one home advantage to every game. A common argument is that a much better visiting team neutralizes the crowd, so home court should count for less when the road team is clearly stronger. If that were true, road favorites would beat their forecast margin, and by more as the gap grows.

## First look

The first two seasons checked pointed in opposite directions. In 2022-23, road favorites won by less than predicted. In early 2023-24, they won by more. Two seasons were not enough to tell, so the question was rerun on the full walk-forward backtest.

## What it shows

- **By size of gap there is no pattern.** Small, medium and large road edges all finished within half a point of the forecast. That is the opposite of what the hypothesis predicts: the gap should widen as the visitor's edge grows.
- **2022-23 stands out.** Road favorites fell 3.2 points short of their forecast (±0.6 standard error). It was the first backtest season, and its model was trained on only one earlier season (2021-22). That is the more likely explanation than anything about home court.
- From 2023-24 on, road favorites beat their forecast by 0.1 to 0.9 points, each within about 1.3 standard errors of zero.

## Next

The evidence is against a strength-dependent home advantage. The question stays open for one narrower check: whether the effect differs in the playoffs, where crowds and stakes are different. The backtest has only about 80 to 90 playoff games per season, so it may need several more seasons to answer.
