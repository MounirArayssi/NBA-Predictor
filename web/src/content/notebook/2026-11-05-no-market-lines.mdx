## What it used to do

Earlier versions used the betting market in several places. They blended the predicted total toward the Vegas total, set a floor on the margin from the spread, used the spread to break close calls, and scored "reliability" by agreement with the line.

Each of those made the forecasts look better, because the market is a very good forecaster. They also made the model partly a copy of the market. That leaves two problems:

1. **It is circular.** A model that moves toward the line cannot then be measured against the line. Any agreement is built in.
2. **It hides what the model knows.** If the forecast is 70% market, you cannot tell whether the other 30% adds anything.

## The change

All of those paths now sit behind a single switch in the forecast code, and the switch is off. That makes the change easy to audit: turning it back on would restore the old behavior exactly.

Lines are still loaded and stored with each forecast, but only so it can be graded against the market afterwards. None of the 21 model inputs comes from a sportsbook.

Now that the comparison is fair, closing that gap is a goal that can actually be measured. It is far too small a sample to draw conclusions from yet.

## Decision

Kept. Market lines are an external benchmark only.
